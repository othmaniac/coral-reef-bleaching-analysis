# Coral Reef Bleaching & Ocean Temperature Analysis

## Overview
This project analyzes historical coral reef bleaching and ocean temperature data to explore how rising sea surface temperatures and heat stress relate to coral bleaching patterns around the world.

The project combines data cleaning, analysis, visualization, and an interactive Tableau dashboard to examine both temporal and geographic patterns in coral reef bleaching.

## Research Question
Can historical coral bleaching and heat-stress data be used to identify reef regions that may face greater future bleaching risk?

## Datasets

### NOAA Coral Reef Watch
NOAA data was used to analyze historical ocean temperature conditions and heat stress.

Variables used in the analysis include:
- Sea Surface Temperature (SST)
- Sea Surface Temperature Anomaly
- Time
- Latitude
- Longitude

### Coral Bleaching Dataset
A separate coral bleaching dataset was used to examine historical bleaching observations and environmental conditions across different geographic regions.

## Data Preparation
The data preparation process included:

- Removing or handling missing values
- Converting temperature measurements when necessary
- Organizing observations by year and geographic region
- Calculating yearly average sea surface temperatures
- Calculating yearly average temperature anomalies
- Preparing cleaned datasets for visualization and analysis

## Analysis & Visualizations
Several visualizations were created to examine coral bleaching and ocean temperature trends:

- Sea surface temperature trends over time
- Temperature anomaly trends over time
- Coral bleaching observations by geographic region
- Interactive geographic visualization of bleaching observations
- Comparison of bleaching patterns across ocean regions

## Tableau Dashboard

An interactive Tableau visualization was created to explore coral reef bleaching geographically.

**[View the Interactive Tableau Visualization](https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/othmane.kerfal/viz/Coral_reefs_Bleaching/Sheet1?publish=yes)**

## Key Findings
The analysis showed an overall increase in ocean temperature and temperature anomalies over time, with noticeable increases after 2015.

Geographic analysis also showed differences in the number of recorded bleaching observations across ocean regions, with the Pacific containing the highest number of observations in the analyzed bleaching dataset.

These patterns demonstrate how historical temperature and bleaching data can be used to examine areas potentially exposed to greater coral bleaching risk.
